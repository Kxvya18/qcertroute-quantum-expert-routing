# Evidence Audit

## What the experiments establish

- The empirical LANL protocol contains 45 de-duplicated 15-minute red-team episodes and retains the hidden first destination endpoint in the active candidate set for all 45 episodes when the full active candidate set is used.
- The rolling evaluation uses four chronological folds, 20 distinct held-out episodes, and five model seeds. Seeds are not independent incident samples.
- In the controlled generalized-preferential-attachment study, fitted exponents recover the generating concentration regime and curvature-prioritized acquisition improves source ranking when source selection follows the same concentration mechanism.

## What the experiments do not establish

- The current LANL auth and DNS summary features do not provide a practically meaningful curvature-policy improvement over entropy acquisition. The apparent AUBC difference is extremely small and must not be used as a central performance claim.
- LANL red-team labels identify a sequence of injected events. The first destination endpoint is an evaluation label, not a verified real-world intrusion origin.
- The controlled results are semi-synthetic mechanism validation. They cannot be presented as operational enterprise incident validation.

## Submission-safe claim

Curvature is a valid local audit of the fitted endpoint-choice mechanism and, under controlled concentrated-growth conditions, supports budgeted structural prioritization. On the current LANL auth and DNS extraction, this mechanism is feasible to evaluate but does not yet yield a practical active-telemetry gain. A publishable empirical claim requires richer query modalities, more incidents, or a dataset with independent ground-truth incident origins.
